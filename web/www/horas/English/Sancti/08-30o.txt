Saints Felix and Adauctus, Martyrs

[Oratio]
We humbly beseech Thy majesty, O Lord: that, even as Thou dost continually gladden us by the commemoration of Thy Saints, so Thou mayest always defend us by their intercession.
$Per Dominum

[Lectio93]
Felix, in the reign of the Emperors Diocletian and Maximian, having been apprehended for having embraced the religion of Christ, was brought into the temple of Serapis. When he was commanded to sacrifice unto it, he spat upon the mouth of the idol. At which deed the brazen statue immediately fell down. Which when it had been done a second and a third time in the shrine of Mercury and of Diana, he was accused of impiety and of magical art, and was tortured upon the rack. Presently he is led to the second milestone from the City on the Ostian Way, that he might be struck with the axe. To whom on the way a certain Christian presented himself, who, recognising Felix and seeing him led to martyrdom, said with a clear voice: I also live by the same law as this man, I worship the same Jesus Christ. And so, having kissed Felix, he is struck with the axe together with him, on the third day before the Kalends of September. Whose name, since it was unknown to the Christians, was ennobled by the name of Adauctus, because he was added unto the holy Martyr Felix for the crown.
