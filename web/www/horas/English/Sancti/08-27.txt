[Officium]
Saint Joseph Calasanctius, Confessor  

[Oratio]
O God, who through Saint Joseph Thy Confessor didst deign to provide a new help for Thy Church for the instructing of youth in the spirit of understanding and of piety: grant, we beseech Thee, that by his example and intercession we may so do and teach that we may obtain eternal rewards.
$Per Dominum

[Lectio4]
Joseph Calasanctius of the Mother of God, born of a noble family at Petralta in Aragon, (on the 15th day of September, in the year of Christ 1556.) from his tender years gave indications of his future charity toward boys and of their instruction. For while yet a little child he would gather them unto himself and instruct them in the mysteries of the faith and in sacred prayers. Having been excellently instructed in human and divine letters, when he was applying himself to theological studies at Valencia, having strongly overcome the allurements of a noble and powerful woman, he preserved inviolate by a notable victory the virginity which he had vowed unto God. Having been made a priest according to his vow, having been taken into a share of the labour by several bishops in the kingdoms of New Castile, Aragon, and Catalonia, he surpassed the expectation of all, the evil manners having been everywhere amended, ecclesiastical discipline restored, and bloody enmities and factions marvellously extinguished. But having been frequently admonished by a heavenly vision and by the voice of God, he set out for Rome.

[Lectio5]
In the City [of Rome], afflicting his body with the greatest austerity of life, with watchings and fastings, he spent days and nights in prayers and in the contemplation of heavenly things, being accustomed almost every night to visit the seven churches of the same City: which custom he kept for many years thereafter. Having given his name to pious sodalities, it is marvellous with how great ardour he would relieve the poor, especially the sick, or those detained in prisons, with alms and every office of piety. When a pestilence was laying waste the City [of Rome], together with Saint Camillus he was driven by so great an impulse of charity that, besides the abundant helps bestowed upon the sick poor, he would even carry upon his own shoulders the very bodies of the dead to be buried. But when he had received from heaven that he was destined for the forming of youths, especially the poor, in the spirit of understanding and of piety, he founded the Order of Clerics Regular of the Poor of the Mother of God of the Pious Schools, who by their own institute should profess a special care concerning the instruction of boys: and this Order, greatly approved by Clement the Eighth, by Paul the Fifth, and by other Supreme Pontiffs, he marvellously propagated in a short time through very many provinces and kingdoms of Europe. But in this he endured so many labours, and bore so many hardships with an invincible mind, that by the voice of all he was called a miracle of fortitude and an example of holy Job.

[Lectio6]
Although he was set over the whole Order, and applied all his strength unto the salvation of souls, yet he never intermitted instructing the boys, especially the poorer ones, whose schools he was accustomed to sweep, and to accompany them home. In this office of the greatest patience and humility he persevered fifty-two years, even in infirm health: worthy therefore that God should illustrate him with frequent miracles in the presence of his disciples, and that the most blessed Virgin with the Child Jesus should appear to him, blessing them while they prayed. Meanwhile, having repudiated the most ample dignities [preferments], he was famous for the gift of prophecy, of knowing the secrets of hearts and things absent [distant events], and for miracles, and having been honoured with frequent sights of the Mother of God, whom he himself from infancy had honoured with singular piety and had especially recommended to his own disciples, and [honoured with sight] of other heavenly ones. When he had foretold the day of his death and the restoration and increase of the Order which then had almost been overthrown, in the ninety-second year of his age he fell asleep in the Lord at Rome, on the eighth of the Kalends of September [25th of August], in the year one thousand six hundred and forty-eight. His heart and tongue after a century were found entire and incorrupt. Also he himself, being illustrated by God also after death with many signs, was first adorned with the Cultus of the Blessed by [Pope] Benedict the Fourteenth, and thereafter was solemnly enrolled among the Saints by [Pope] Clement the Thirteenth.

[Lectio94]
Joseph Calasanctius, born at Petralta in Aragon, while yet a little child would gather his equals unto himself and instruct them in the mysteries of the faith and in sacred prayers. Having been made a priest according to his vow, afflicting his body with the greatest austerity of life, with watchings and fastings, he spent days and nights in prayer and in the contemplation of heavenly things. When he had received from heaven that he was destined for the forming of youths, especially the poor, in the spirit of understanding and of piety, he founded the Order of Clerics Regular of the Poor of the Mother of God of the Pious Schools, who by their own institute should profess a special care concerning the instruction of boys. Having therefore endured innumerable labours and hardships with an invincible mind, in the ninety-second year of his age he died in the Lord, on the eighth of the Kalends of September, in the year one thousand six hundred and forty-eight.

Homily of Saint John Chrysostom.
!On chapter 18 of Matthew, Homily 60. 
See that ye despise not one of these little ones, because their angels always see the face of My Father, and because I came for them, and this is the will of My Father. He rendereth us more diligent for the guarding and preserving of the little ones. Thou seest how great walls He hath raised for the protection of the weak, and how great study and care He hath that they be not lost: both because He hath appointed the supreme punishments for those who despise them, and because He promiseth the highest reward unto those who take care of them; and this He confirmeth both by His own example and by that of the Father.

[Lectio8]
Let us therefore also imitate the Lord, and omit nothing for the brethren, even of those things [good works] which seem very humble and mean; but if there shall be need even of our administration, although he to whom it is to be administered be lowly [thin] and abject [despised], although the matter seem to us hard and full of labour, I pray that all these things may appear more tolerable and easier for the salvation of a brother: for with so great study and so great care hath God shown the soul to be worthy, that He spared not even His own Son.

[Lectio9]
If it is not enough for us for our salvation that we ourselves live virtuously, but that we ought in very deed to desire the salvation of others; what then when neither we ourselves live rightly, nor exhort others, what shall we answer? What hope of salvation shall remain for us? What is greater [for attaining salvation] than to govern [direct] souls, than to shape [or sculpt] the manners of youths? I certainly hold him to be more excellent than every painter, than every sculptor, and all others of this kind, who knoweth how to shape [sculpt] the souls of the young.
