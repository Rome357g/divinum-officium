[Hymnus Vespera]
v. Now let sudden evening spread over all the sky,
And let the sun cast down the astonished day,
While I recall the mockery of the cruel death,
And the divine catastrophe.
_
Thou wast present as a spectator at the punishment, O Mother,
Wet with sorrows, bearing a heart of adamant;
While thy Son hanging upon the deathly cross
Gave forth deep groans.
_
Thy Son hanging before thine eyes,
Cut with atrocious stripes,
Thy Son pierced with gaping wounds
With how many penetrating darts did He pierce thee!
_
Alas! Spittings, blows, stripes, wounds,
Nails, gall, aloes, sponge, lance,
Thirst, thorn, blood, with how various a tyranny
Did they press upon thy pious heart!
_
Meanwhile thou, O Virgin, standest more noble than all the Martyrs:
By a new wonder, dying amidst such great woes,
Thou diest not, O Mother,
Pierced with dreadful sorrows.
_
Glory, praise, honour be to the most high Trinity,
From whom, as a suppliant, with anxious prayer,
I ask strength rivalling virginal fortitude
In hard affairs.
Amen.

[Hymnus Matutinum]
v. O with how many waves of tears,
With what sorrow is she overwhelmed,
While the mournful Virgin Mother beholdeth her Son,
Torn from the bloody tree,
Lying in her arms!
_
The sweet mouth, the gentle breast,
And the most sweet side,
And the wounded right hand
And the injured left,
And the feet red with blood
She, sorrowful, bathes with tears.
_
A hundred times and a thousand times
She bindeth that breast and arms in tight embraces,
She imprinteth kisses on those wounds:
And so she wholly melts away
In kisses of sorrow.
_
Come, O Mother, we beseech thee
By these thy tears,
And the sad death of thy Son,
and the purple of His wounds,
Hide this sorrow of thy heart
In our hearts.
_
Be to the Father, and to the Son,
And to the coeternal Flame,
Be to the most high Trinity
Everlasting glory,
And perennial praise and honour
In this and every age.
Amen.

Homily of Saint Ambrose the Bishop
!On the Institution of a Virgin, chapter 7
The Mother stood by the cross, and, while the men fled, she stood intrepid. See whether the Mother of Jesus could alter her modesty, who altered not her mind. She looked with pious eyes upon the wounds of her Son, through which she knew redemption would come to all. She looked, a mother upon no degenerate spectacle, who had not feared the destroyer. The Son hung upon the cross, the Mother offered herself to the persecutors.

[Lectio8]
!Epistle 25 to the Church of Vercelli, near the end
Mary the Mother of the Lord stood before the cross of the Son. No one taught me this except the holy Evangelist John. Others have written that the world was shaken in the passion of the Lord, that the sky (or heaven) was covered with darkness, that the sun fled, that the thief was received into paradise, but after a pious confession (of sin). John taught what others did not teach, how, when placed upon the cross, He called His Mother. It is thought of greater moment that the victor over torments showed the offices of piety to His Mother, than that He gave the heavenly kingdom. For if it is religious that pardon is given to the thief, it is of much richer piety that the Mother is honoured by the Son with such affection.

[Lectio9]
Behold, He saith, thy son: behold thy mother. Christ testified from the cross, and divided the duties of piety between His mother and the disciple. The Lord was making not only a public, but also a domestic testament: and John was signing this testament of His, a witness worthy of so great a testator. A good testament, not of money, but of eternal life; which is written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God, who saith: My tongue is the pen of a scrivener that writeth swiftly.

The Seven Sorrows of the Virgin

Of the five wounds of Jesus,
