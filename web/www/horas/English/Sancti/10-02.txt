[Lectio4]
Sermon of Saint Bernard the Abbot
!On the Psalm Who dwelleth (90)
He hath given His Angels charge over thee. Wondrous condescension, and truly a great love of charity. For who? to whom? concerning whom? what hath he commanded? Let us studiously consider, brethren, let us diligently commit to memory so great a commandment. For who hath commanded? whose are the Angels? whose commandments do they obey? whose will do they obey? Truly He hath given His Angels charge over thee, that they keep thee in all thy ways. Nor do they hesitate even to bear thee up in their hands. The highest Majesty therefore hath commanded the Angels, and hath commanded His own Angels. Unto those sublime ones indeed, as blessed as they are intimately united to Himself and truly of His household, He hath given charge concerning thee. Who art thou? What is man, that Thou art mindful of him? or the son of man, that Thou considerest him? As if indeed man were not rottenness, and the son of man a worm! But what thinkest thou He hath commanded concerning thee? That they keep thee.

[Lectio5]
How much reverence ought this word instill in thee, bring devotion, confer confidence! Reverence for their presence, devotion for their benevolence, confidence for their custody. Walk carefully, as one unto whom Angels are present, as it hath been commanded them, in all thy ways. In every lodging, in every corner, have reverence for thy Angel. Dare not thou in his presence, what thou wouldst not dare in my sight. Or dost thou doubt him to be present, whom thou dost not see? What if thou shouldst hear him? what if thou shouldst touch him? what if thou shouldst smell him? See, that the presence of things is not proven by sight alone.

[Lectio6]
In Him therefore, brethren, let us affectionately love His Angels, as those who are to be some day our co-heirs, but meanwhile agents and tutors, set and appointed by the Father over us. What should we fear under such guardians? They can neither be overcome nor deceived, much less can they deceive, who keep us in all our ways. They are faithful, they are prudent, they are powerful: why do we tremble? Only let us follow them, let us adhere to them, and let us abide in the protection of the God of heaven. As often therefore as a most grievous temptation is seen to press and a vehement tribulation to threaten, invoke thy guardian, thy leader, thy helper in due times, in tribulation; cry out to him and say: Lord, save us, we perish.

Homily of Saint Hilary the Bishop
!Commentary on Matthew, canon 18, after the beginning
The Lord teacheth that none but those who have returned to the nature of children shall enter the kingdom of heaven: that is, the vices of our bodies and of our soul are to be withdrawn through childish simplicity. But He hath called all believers children through the faith of hearing. For these follow their father, they love their mother, they know not how to wish evil to their neighbor, they neglect the care of riches; they do not become insolent, they hate not, they lie not, they believe what is said, and what they hear, they hold as true. We must return therefore to the simplicity of infants; because, being placed in it, we shall carry about the image of the Lord's humility.

[Lectio8]
Woe to this world because of scandals. The humility of the passion is a scandal to the world. For in this especially is human ignorance detained, that beneath the deformity of the cross, it would not receive the Lord of eternal glory. And what is so dangerous to the world, as not to have received Christ? Therefore truly He saith that it is necessary that scandals come; because, for the sacrament of restoring eternity to us, all the humility of the passion was to be fulfilled in Him.

[Lectio9]
See that you despise not one of these little ones, who believe in Me. Most fittingly hath He imposed the bond of mutual love, especially toward those who had truly believed in the Lord. For the Angels of the little ones daily see God: because the Son of man is come to save that which was lost. Therefore both the Son of man saveth, and the Angels see God, and the Angels of the little ones preside over the prayers of the faithful. That the Angels preside is absolute authority. Therefore the Angels daily offer to God the prayers of those saved through Christ. Dangerously therefore is he contemned, whose desires and petitions are carried to the eternal and invisible God by the earnest servitude and ministry of the Angels.

v. O Eternal Ruler of the stars
Who hast created whatever is by Thy great power,
And rulest it with no less providence:
_
Be present to the assembly of the guilty supplicating Thee,
And beneath the twilight of the light
Give new light to our minds.
_
And may Thy Angel, chosen for our custody,
Be present here;
That he may protect us from the contagion of crimes.
_

Lest he weave the snare of fraud

_
