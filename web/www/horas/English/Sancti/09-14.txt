[Lectio4]
Chosroes, king of the Persians, in the last times of the empire of Phocas, having occupied Egypt and Africa and taken Jerusalem (in 614), and having slain many thousands of Christians there, carried away into Persia the Cross of Christ the Lord, which Helena had placed upon Mount Calvary. Wherefore Heraclius, who had succeeded Phocas, being afflicted with many disadvantages and calamities of war, sought peace; which from Chosroes, insolent with victories, he could not obtain even on unjust conditions. Wherefore in the greatest danger, exercising himself with assiduous fasts and prayers, he earnestly implored help from God; by whose monition, having prepared an army, he joined battle with the enemy, and overcame three generals of Chosroes with three armies.

[Lectio5]
Broken by these disasters, Chosroes, in the flight in which he was preparing to cross the Tigris, designates Medarses his son as partner of the kingdom (in 628). But Siroes, the elder son of Chosroes, bearing this contumely with rage, plots the death of both father and brother; which a little later he inflicted upon both, drawn back from flight, and obtained the kingdom from Heraclius upon certain accepted conditions, of which the first was that he should restore the Cross of Christ the Lord. Therefore the Cross, fourteen years after it had come into the power of the Persians, was recovered (in 629). Which returning to Jerusalem, Heraclius with solemn celebration carried upon his own shoulders to that mountain whither the Saviour had borne it.

[Lectio6]
Which deed was recommended by an illustrious miracle. For Heraclius, as he was adorned with gold and gems, was compelled to stand still at the gate which leads to Mount Calvary. For the more he strove to advance, the more he seemed to be held back. And when both Heraclius himself and all the rest were astonished at this, Zacharias, bishop of Jerusalem, said: See, O emperor, lest in this triumphal attire, in bearing the Cross, thou imitate too little the poverty and humility of Jesus Christ. Then Heraclius, having cast off the most ample vesture and having drawn off his shoes and put on a common garment, easily completed the rest of the way, and set the Cross in the same place of Calvary whence it had been carried away by the Persians. And so the solemnity of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross, which was celebrated yearly on this day, began to be held more illustrious on account of the memory of that event, that it was there replaced by Heraclius where it had first been set up for the Saviour.

Homily of Saint Leo the Pope
!Sermon 8 on the Passion of the Lord, after the middle
The Cross being exalted, beloved, let not only that appearance present itself to the sight of the mind which was before the eyes of the ungodly, to whom it was said by Moses: And thy life shall be as it were hanging before thine eyes, and thou shalt fear day and night, and shalt not believe thy life. For these could think of nothing in the crucified Lord except their own crime, having a fear, not that by which true faith is justified, but that by which an iniquitous conscience is tormented. But let our understanding, which the spirit of truth illuminates, receive with pure and free heart the glory of the Cross, radiating heaven and earth; and with interior vision see of what sort is that which the Lord, when He spoke of the nearness of His passion, said: Now is the judgment of the world, now shall the prince of this world be cast out. And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all things to Myself.

[Lectio8]
O admirable power of the Cross! O ineffable glory of the Passion, in which is both the tribunal of the Lord, and the judgment of the world, and the power of the Crucified! For Thou hast drawn all things to Thyself, O Lord, and when Thou hadst stretched out Thy hands all the day to a people that believeth not and contradicteth Thee, the whole world received the sense of confessing Thy majesty. Thou hast drawn all things to Thyself, O Lord, when for the execration of the Jewish crime all the elements put forth one sentence; when, the luminaries of heaven being darkened and the day turned into night, the earth also was shaken with unwonted motions, and all creation denied itself to the use of the ungodly. Thou hast drawn all things to Thyself, O Lord, because, the veil of the temple being rent, the Holy of Holies withdrew from the unworthy priests; that the figure might be turned into truth, prophecy into manifestation, and the law into the Gospel.

[Lectio9]
Thou hast drawn all things to Thyself, O Lord, that what was covered in one temple of Judaea with shadowed significations, the devotion of all nations everywhere might celebrate in a full and open sacrament. For now also is the order of levites clearer, and the dignity of elders ampler, and the unction of priests more sacred: because Thy Cross is the fountain of all blessings, the cause of all graces; through which to those that believe is given strength from weakness, glory from reproach, life from death. Now also, the variety of carnal sacrifices having ceased, one oblation of Thy Body and Blood fills up all the differences of victims: because Thou art the true Lamb of God, who takest away the sins of the world; and so in Thyself Thou perfectest all mysteries, that as there is one sacrifice for every victim, so there is one kingdom from every nation.
