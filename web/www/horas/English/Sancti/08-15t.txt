Sermon of Saint John Damascene
!Oration 2 on the Dormition of the Blessed Virgin Mary, after the beginning
This day the sacred and living Ark of the living God, who conceived her Creator in her womb, resteth in the temple of the Lord, which was built by no hands. And David her father rejoiceth, and with him the Angels lead the dances, the Archangels celebrate, the Virtues glorify, the Principalities exult, the Powers rejoice together, the Dominations are glad, the Thrones keep festival, the Cherubim praise, the Seraphim proclaim her glory. This day Eden receiveth the living paradise of the new Adam, in which the condemnation was loosed, in which the Tree of Life was planted, in which our nakedness was covered.

[Lectio5_]
This day the immaculate Virgin, who was defiled by no earthly affections, but was lead out by heavenly thoughts, hath not returned unto the earth; but, being a living heaven, is placed in the heavenly tabernacles. For from whom true life flowed forth unto all, how should she taste death? But she yieldeth to the law laid down by Him whom she bore; and, as daughter of the old Adam, she underwent the ancient sentence (for her Son also, who is life itself, did not refuse it); but as Mother of the living God, she is worthily assumed unto Him Himself.

[Lectio6_]
Eve, who gave assent to the suggestion of the serpent, is condemned to the pain of childbirth and to the sentence of death, and is placed in the inmost parts of the underworld. But this truly blessed one, who lent her ears to the word of God, and was filled with the operation of the Holy Spirit, and at the spiritual salutation of the Archangel, without pleasure and without the company of man, conceived the Son of God, and without any pain brought Him forth, and consecrated her whole self unto God, how should death devour her? how should the underworld receive her? how should corruption invade that body in which life was received? For her a straight, plain, and easy way hath been prepared unto heaven. For if, Where I am, there also shall My minister be, saith Christ the life and the truth; how much rather shall the mother be with Him?

Homily of Saint Augustine the Bishop (of Hippo.)
!Sermon 27 on the Words of the Lord, tome 10
When the holy Gospel was being read, we heard that the Lord was received as a guest by a religious woman, and she was called Martha. And when she herself was occupied in the care of ministering, her sister Mary sat at the feet of the Lord, and heard His word. The one was labouring, the other was at leisure. The one was distributing, this one was being filled. Nevertheless Martha, labouring much in that occupation and business of ministering, interrupted the Lord: and complained of her sister, that she did not help her as she laboured.

[Lectio8]
But the Lord answered Martha for Mary: and He Himself became her advocate, who had been appealed to as judge. Martha, He saith, thou art occupied about many things, when one thing is necessary. Mary hath chosen the better part, which shall not be taken away from her. For we have heard both the interruption of the one interrupting, and the sentence of the judge. Which sentence answered the one interrupting, and defended the one received. For Mary was intent upon the sweetness of the word of the Lord. Martha was intent upon how she might feed the Lord: Mary was intent upon how she might be fed by the Lord. By Martha a banquet was being prepared for the Lord, in whose banquet Mary was already rejoicing.

[Lectio9]
When therefore Mary was sweetly hearing the most sweet word, and was being fed with a most intent heart, the Lord having been interrupted by her sister, how do we think she feared, lest the Lord should say unto her: Arise, and help thy sister? For she was held by a wondrous sweetness: which is assuredly greater of the mind than of the belly. She was excused, she sat more securely. But how was she excused? Let us attend, let us look into, let us search out what we can, that we also may be fed.
