Our Lady of Ransom;;Simplex;;1.1;;ex C11

[Oratio]
O God, who through the most glorious Mother of Thy Son, for delivering the faithful of Christ from the power of the pagans, didst deign to amplify Thy Church with a new offspring: grant, we beseech Thee; that we who piously venerate the foundress of so great a work, may likewise by her merits and intercession, be delivered from all sins and the captivity of the devil.
$Per eumdem

[Lectio4]
At the time when the greater and more prosperous part of the Spains was oppressed by the dire yoke of the Saracens, and countless faithful were unhappily detained under cruel servitude, with the greatest danger of abjuring the Christian faith, and of losing eternal salvation, the most blessed Queen of heaven, benignly coming to meet so many and such great evils, showed her exceeding great charity in redeeming them. For to Saint Peter Nolasco, flourishing in piety and riches, who, given to holy meditations, continually considered in his mind by what means succour might be brought to the hardships of so many Christians living under the captivity of the Moors, the most blessed Virgin herself with serene countenance gave herself to be seen; and said that it would be most acceptable to herself and to her only-begotten Son if in her honour an order of religious were instituted, upon whom should rest the care of freeing captives from the tyranny of the Turks [Moors]. Refreshed by which heavenly vision, it is wonderful with what ardour of charity the man of God began to burn, keeping this one thing in his heart, that he himself and the religion to be instituted by him should diligently exercise that greatest charity, that each one should lay down his soul for his friends and neighbours.

[Lectio5]
On that very night the same most holy Virgin appeared to blessed Raymund of Peñafort and to James king of Aragon, admonishing them of this same thing concerning the instituting of religious men, and persuading them that they should bring help for the construction of so great a work. But Peter, immediately flying to the feet of Raymond, who was his confessor, opened the whole matter to him; whom also he found instructed from heaven, and most humbly subjected himself to his direction. But James the king, coming upon them, determined to execute the revelation which he himself also had received from the most blessed Virgin. Wherefore, counsel having been taken among them, and their minds agreeing, they set about instituting an order in honour of the same Virgin Mother, under the invocation of Saint Mary of Ransom for the redemption of captives.

[Lectio6]
Therefore on the tenth day of August in the year of the Lord one thousand two hundred and eighteen, the same king James determined to execute that institution long since conceived by those holy men, the members being bound by a fourth vow of remaining as a pledge under the power of the pagans, if it should be necessary for the liberation of Christians. To whom the king himself granted that they should bear his royal arms upon their breast, and he caused that institute of so excellent a charity toward one’s neighbour and the religion to be confirmed by Gregory the Ninth. But God Himself also through the Virgin Mother gave increase, so that such an institution might more swiftly and more happily be spread throughout the whole world, and flourish with holy men distinguished by charity and piety, who should expend the alms collected from the faithful of Christ as the price of the redemption of their neighbours, and should sometimes even give themselves for the redemption of many. But that due thanks may be rendered to God and to the Virgin Mother for so great a benefit and institution, the Apostolic See has granted that this special festivity be celebrated and the Office recited, having likewise conferred almost innumerable other privileges upon the same order.

[Lectio94]
At the time when countless faithful were unhappily detained under the cruel servitude of the Saracens, with the danger of losing eternal salvation, the blessed Virgin Mary, appearing by night to Saint Peter Nolasco, to blessed Raymund of Peñafort, and to James king of Aragon, said that it would be most acceptable to herself and to her only-begotten Son if an order of religious men were instituted in her honor, upon whom the care of delivering captives from the tyranny of the Turks should fall. Wherefore, having taken counsel among themselves and with consenting minds, they undertook to institute an order in honor of the same Virgin Mother, under the invocation of Holy Mary of Ransom of the redemption of captives, with the companions bound by a fourth vow of remaining as a pledge under the power of the pagans, if it were necessary for the deliverance of Christians. To whom the king himself granted to bear his own royal arms upon their breast, and he took care that this institution of so excellent a charity be confirmed by Gregory the Ninth. But that due thanks of so great a benefit and institution might be returned to God and the Virgin Mother, the Apostolic See granted this special festivity to be celebrated.
