[Officium]
Saints Euphemia, Lucy and Geminianus, Martyrs

[Oratio]
v. Grant, O Lord, to our prayers an outcome with exultation: that of the holy Martyrs Euphemia, Lucy and Geminianus, whose day of passion we recall with yearly devotion, we may also follow the constancy of faith.
$Per Dominum

[Lectio93]
Euphemia, Lucy and Geminianus, in the persecution of Diocletian, were crowned with martyrdom not in the same place, but on the same day. The virgin Euphemia at Chalcedon, under the proconsul Priscus, constantly endured various kinds of torments—rods, the rack, wheels, and fire; at last, being thrown to the beasts, one of them fixing a bite in her holy body, while the others licked her feet, she rendered up her immaculate spirit to God. Lucy, a Roman widow, accused by her son Eutropius of having worshipped Christ for many years, is cast into a cauldron of boiling pitch and lead; from which escaping unharmed, while she was being led through the City (of Rome) laden with iron and lead, she converted Geminianus, a noble man, to Christ by the constancy of her faith and martyrdom. Whom also, with many others brought to the faith, variously tortured, she had as companion of glorious martyrdom, his head being cut off. Whose bodies Maxima, a Christian woman, buried honourably.
