[Officium]
Saint Francis, Confessor

[Oratio]
O God, who by the merits of blessed Francis dost enlarge Thy Church with the offspring of a new progeny: grant unto us that, by imitating him, we may despise earthly things and ever rejoice in the participation of heavenly gifts.
$Per Dominum

[Lectio4]
Francis, born at Assisi in Umbria, following his father's example, engaged in trade from his early age. One day, when beyond his custom he had refused money to a poor man who begged for Christ’s sake, being suddenly moved by that deed, he bestowed alms upon him largely; and from that day he promised to God that he would never refuse an alms to any that asked. But when afterwards he had fallen into a grave sickness, and was at length recovered from it, he began more ardently to cultivate the offices of charity; in which exercise he so far advanced that, desirous of evangelical perfection, he distributed whatever he had to the poor. His father, taking this amiss, brought him to the Bishop of Assisi, that before him he might renounce his paternal goods; whereupon, casting off even his garments, he yielded all things to his father, adding that thereafter he should have greater freedom to say: Our Father, who art in heaven.
[Lesson 5]

[Lectio5]
But when he had heard that saying from the Gospel: Do not possess gold, nor silver, nor money in your purses, nor scrip for your journey, nor two coats, nor shoes; he proposed that rule to himself to be kept. Therefore, having put off his shoes and content with one tunic, having taken twelve companions, he instituted the Order of Friars Minor. Wherefore he came to Rome in the year of salvation one thousand two hundred and nine, that the rule of his Order might be confirmed by the Apostolic See. Whom, when the Supreme Pontiff Innocent the Third had at first rejected as he approached him; because afterwards in a dream he had seemed to see him whom he had rejected supporting with his shoulders the Lateran basilica that was falling, he ordered him to be sought out and summoned, and receiving him kindly, confirmed the whole manner of his institutes. Francis therefore, having sent the brethren into all parts of the world to preach the Gospel of Christ, himself desiring that some occasion of martyrdom might be given him, sailed into Syria; where, being treated most liberally by the Sultan, when he could profit nothing, he returned into Italy.

[Lectio6]
Having therefore built many dwellings for his family, he withdrew into the solitude of Mount Alvernia; where, having begun a fast of forty days for the honour of Saint Michael the Archangel, on the feast day of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross, there appeared to him a Seraph containing the image of the Crucified between his wings; who impressed upon his hands and feet and side the marks of the nails. Which things Saint Bonaventure, when he was present at the preaching of the Supreme Pontiff Alexander the Fourth, recorded in writing that the Pontiff had related them as having been seen by himself. By these marks of the highest love of Christ toward him, he had the greatest admiration of all. And two years later, being grievously sick, he wished to be carried into the church of Saint Mary of the Angels; that where he had received the spirit of grace from God, there he might render up the spirit of life. In that place, having exhorted the brethren to poverty and patience, and to the keeping of the faith of the holy Roman Church, reciting that Psalm, I have cried to the Lord with my voice [141st]; at the verse, The just wait for me, until Thou reward me, he breathed forth his soul on the fourth of the Nones of October. Whom, renowned for miracles, Gregory the Ninth, Supreme Pontiff, enrolled in the number of the Saints.  

[Lectio94]
Francis was born at Assisi in Umbria. Following the example of his father, from his earliest years he practised merchandise. Being seized with a grave sickness, when he had recovered, he began more ardently to cultivate the offices of charity, and to his father who took it amiss he yielded all his goods, adding that thereafter he should have greater freedom to say: Our Father, who art in heaven. But when he had heard from the Gospel the counsels of apostolic poverty, having put off his shoes and content with one tunic, having taken twelve companions, he instituted the Order of Friars Minor; which, confirmed by the Supreme Pontiff Innocent the Third, who was admonished from heaven, he marvellously propagated. Soon he withdrew into the solitude of Mount Alvernia, and there, on the day of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross, from a Seraph bearing before him the image of the Crucified between his wings, he received the sacred stigmata; the marks of the nails and of the lance appearing in his hands, feet and side. Two years later, being grievously sick, in the church of Saint Mary of the Angels, where he had received the spirit of grace from God, having exhorted the brethren to poverty, patience and the keeping of the faith of the holy Roman Church, he most piously breathed forth his soul on the fourth of the Nones of October.
