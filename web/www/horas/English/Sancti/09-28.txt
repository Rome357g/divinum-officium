[Officium]
Saint Wenceslaus, Duke and Martyr

[Oratio]
O God, who by the palm of martyrdom didst transfer the blessed Wenceslaus from an earthly principality to heavenly glory: by his prayers keep us from all adversity; and grant us to rejoice in his fellowship.
$Per Dominum

[Lectio4]
Wenceslaus, duke of Bohemia, born of a Christian father Wratislaus and a pagan mother Drahomira, was piously educated by his grandmother Ludmilla, a most holy woman, and, being distinguished by every kind of virtue, with the greatest zeal preserved his virginity unspotted throughout his whole life. His mother, having obtained the administration of the realm by the nefarious murder of Ludmilla, living impiously with her younger son Boleslaus, stirred up against herself the indignation of the nobles; wherefore, wearied of the tyrannical and impious government, having shaken off the yoke of both, they saluted Wenceslaus as king in the city of Prague.

[Lectio5]
He, ruling the kingdom more by piety than by power, assisted orphans, widows, and the needy with such charity that he sometimes carried wood by night upon his own shoulders for the indigent, frequently assisted at the burial of the poor, liberated captives, visited those detained in prisons in the dead of night, most often consoling them with money and counsel. The prince of mild soul was vehemently grieved that anyone, even if guilty, should be adjudged to death. With the highest religion he venerated priests, with his own hands he sowed wheat and pressed wine, which they might use in the sacrifice of the Mass. By night with bare feet upon the snow and ice he went around the churches, leaving behind him bloody footprints that warmed the earth.

[Lectio6]
He had angels as the guardians of his body. For when he was approaching single combat against Radislaus, duke of the Curimenses, to that end that he might provide for the safety of his own, angels were seen to have ministered arms and to have said to the adversary: Strike not. The terrified enemy, falling down in reverence, begged pardon. When he had gone into Germany, the Emperor, having beheld two angels adorning him with a golden cross as he approached, leaping from the throne received him in his arms, decorated him with the royal insignia, and gave him the arms of Saint Vitus. Nevertheless his impious brother, at the urging of his mother, having received him at a banquet and afterwards while he was praying in the church, knowing beforehand of the death prepared for him, with the accomplices of the crime admitted, slew him. The blood sprinkled upon the walls is still to be seen, and, God being the avenger, the earth swallowed up the inhuman mother, and the murderers perished miserably in various ways.

His mother, having obtained the administration of the realm by the nefarious murder of Ludmilla, living impiously with her younger son Boleslaus, stirred up against herself the indignation of the nobles; wherefore, wearied of the impious government, having shaken off the yoke of both, they saluted Wenceslaus as king in the city of Prague. Who governed the kingdom more by piety than by power, skilled and assiduous in relieving the needy and the afflicted. With the highest religion he venerated priests, with his own hands he sowed wheat and pressed wine to be used in the sacrifice of the Mass. But when he had been decorated by the emperor with the royal insignia, he was slain by his impious brother, at the persuasion of his mother, while praying in the church. His blood sprinkled upon the walls is still to be seen.

!Matt 10:34-42
At that time: Jesus said to His disciples: Do not think that I came to send peace upon earth: I came not to send peace, but the sword. And the rest.
_
Homily of Saint Hilary the Bishop
!Comment. on Matth., can. 10
What is this division? For among the first precepts of the law we have received: Honour thy father and thy mother; and the Lord Himself saith: My peace I give unto you, My peace I leave with you. What meaneth it that a sword rather is sent upon the earth, and a son is separated from the father, and a daughter from the mother, and a daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law, and a man’s household are his enemies? Therefore from thence public authority will be put forth for impiety. Everywhere hatreds, everywhere wars, and the sword of the Lord raging between father and son, and between daughter and mother.

[Lectio8]
The sword is the sharpest of all weapons, in which is the right of power, and the severity of judgment, and the chastisement of sins. And, under the name of this weapon indeed, the preaching of the new Gospel is frequently called by the authority in the prophets. Let us therefore remember that the word of God is named in the sword; which sword is sent upon the earth, that is, its preaching infused into the hearts of men. And there is made a grievous dissension in one house, and the household will be enemies to the new man; because he, divided from them by the word of God, will rejoice to remain both interior and exterior, that is, both body and soul, in the newness of the spirit.

[Lectio9]
He proceeds thereafter by the same course of precepts and understanding. For after He had commanded that all things which are most dear in the world are to be left, He added: He that taketh not up his cross, and followeth Me, is not worthy of Me; because They that are Christ’s have crucified the flesh with the vices and concupiscences. And he is unworthy of Christ who, not taking up his cross, in which we suffer together, die together, are buried together, rise again together, is not secure in following the Lord, about to live in this sacrament of faith, in the newness of the spirit.
