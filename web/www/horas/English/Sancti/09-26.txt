[Officium]
Saints Cyprian and Justina, Virgin, Martyrs

[Oratio]
May the continual protections of the blessed Martyrs Cyprian and Justina foster us, O Lord: for Thou ceasest not to look propitiously upon those whom Thou hast granted to be helped by such aids.
$Per Dominum

[Lectio2]
Cyprian, at first a magician, afterwards a Martyr, when he attempted by incantations and sorceries to entice Justina, a Christian Virgin, whom a certain young man ardently loved, unto assent to his lust, consulted a demon by what means he might accomplish this. To whom the demon answered that no art would avail him against those who truly worshiped Christ. Cyprian, moved by this answer, began vehemently to grieve over the conduct of his former life. Therefore, having abandoned the magical arts, he converted himself wholly to the faith of Christ the Lord.

[Lectio3]
For which cause he was apprehended together with the Virgin Justina, and both were beaten with buffets and scourges; presently they were cast into prison, if perchance they might change their resolve. But being afterwards let go from thence, when they were found most constant in the Christian religion, they were cast into a frying pan full of boiling pitch, fat, and wax. At length at Nicomedia they were beheaded. Whose cast-out bodies, when they had lain unburied for six days, certain sailors by night secretly placed upon a ship and carried to Rome. And at first they were buried on the estate of Rufina, a noble woman; afterwards, translated into the City, they were enshrined in the Constantinian basilica near the baptistery.
