Saints Placidus and Companions, Martyrs  

[Name]
Placidus and his Companions

[Lectio93]
Placidus was born at Rome of Tertullus, a father of the first nobility. Being offered to God as a boy and delivered to Saint Benedict, he so far advanced by his discipline and by the institutes of the monastic life that he is numbered among his principal disciples. Being sent by him into Sicily, he built a monastery and a church in honour of Saint John the Baptist near the harbour of Messina, where he lived with the monks in admirable sanctity. When Eutychius and Victorinus, his brothers, and Flavia, his virgin sister, had come thither for the purpose of visiting him, there arrived at the same time a certain fierce pirate named Manucha; who, having taken the monastery, when he could by no means induce Placidus and the others to deny Christ, ordered him and his brothers and sister to be cruelly slain. With whom also Donatus, Firmatus the deacon, Faustus and thirty other monks happily consummated the combat of martyrdom on the third of the Nones of October, in the year of salvation five hundred and thirty-nine.
