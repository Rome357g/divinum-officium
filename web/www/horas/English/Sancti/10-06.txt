[Officium]
Saint Bruno, Confessor

[Oratio]
We beseech Thee, O Lord, that we may be helped by the intercessions of Saint Bruno Thy Confessor: that we who have grievously offended Thy majesty by sinning, may by his merits and prayers obtain pardon of our offences.  
$Per Dominum

[Lectio4]
Bruno, the founder of the Carthusian Order, [Or: Charterhouse Monks] was born at Cologne. [about 1030] From his very cradle showing a pattern of future sanctity, and by the gravity of his manners, with the favour of divine grace, declining the childish things of that age, he so far excelled that already from thence he was recognised as a father of monks and the future restorer of the anachoretical life. Being sent by his parents, who were distinguished by birth and virtue, to Paris, he so far advanced there in the studies of philosophy and theology that he obtained the office of doctor and master in both faculties; and not long after, on account of his outstanding virtues, he obtained a canonry of the Church of Rheims.  

[Lectio5]
Some years having elapsed, renouncing the world with six other companions, he went to Saint Hugh, Bishop of Grenoble. Who, having learned the cause of their coming, and understanding them to be those whom on the same night he had seen in a dream as seven stars falling at his feet, granted to them the roughest mountains of his diocese, which are called the Chartreuse [whence comes Carthusian or Charterhouse]. Thither Bruno, withdrawing with his companions, Hugh himself accompanying them, when he had led an eremitical life for some years, was summoned to Rome by Urban the Second [1089], who had been a disciple of the same Bruno. The Pontiff used his counsel and doctrine for some years in those many calamities of the Church; until Bruno, having refused the archbishopric of Reggio, obtained leave to depart.

[Lectio6]
Therefore, through love of solitude, he sought a certain wilderness near Squillace in the borders of Calabria [in 1090]. In which place, when Roger, Count of Calabria, while hunting, had found him praying, the dogs barking at his cave, being moved by the sanctity of the man, he began earnestly to foster and honour him and his companions. Nor was the liberality without reward; for when the same Roger was besieging Capua, and a certain Sergius, master of the watch, had decided to betray him, Bruno, still living in the said hermitage, revealing all things to him in dreams, delivered the count from imminent danger. At length, full of virtues and merits, and no less renowned for sanctity than for the fame of his doctrine, he fell asleep in the Lord [in 1101]; and was buried in the monastery of Saint Stephen, built by Roger himself, where he is still honourably venerated.  

[Lectio94]
Bruno, the founder of the Carthusian Order, was born at Cologne. From his boyhood he excelled by the gravity of his manners and the desire of solitude. Being sent by his parents to Paris, he so far advanced there in the studies of philosophy and theology that he obtained the office of doctor and master in both faculties; and not long after, on account of his outstanding virtues, he obtained a canonry of the Church of Rheims. Having instituted the Order of Carthusians, when he had led an eremitical life in it for some years, he was summoned to Rome by Urban the Second, who had been his disciple. The Pontiff used his counsel and doctrine for some years in the many calamities of that time; until the man of God, having refused the archbishopric of Reggio and obtained leave to depart, again sought the wilderness, where, full of virtues and merits, he fell asleep in the Lord.
