[Officium]
Sts. Timothy, Hippolytus and Symphorianus, Martyrs

[Oratio]
Mercifully help us, O Lord, we beseech thee, and at the pleading of thine holy Martyrs Timothy, Hippolytus, and Symphorian, stretch forth over us the right hand of thy favour.
$Per Dominum

[Lectio93]
Timothy came from Antioch to Rome in the time of Pope Melchiades. He had preached the faith of Christ there for a year, when he was thrown into irons by Tarquinius, Praefect of the city. After suffering a long imprisonment he was brought to the idols to offer them sacrifice. He refused right boldly to commit this great sin, and was thereupon savagely scourged, and his raw body covered with quick-lime. He steadily persisted in his testimony under these and other tortures, and at last was beheaded, (in the year 311.) His body was buried on the Ostian Way near the sepulchre of blessed Paul the Apostle. On which day also, (in the year 180,) in the time of the Emperor Alexander, at Ostia on the Tiber, Hippolytus, Bishop of Porto, on account of his outstanding confession of the faith, having his hands and feet bound, was cast down into a deep pit filled with water, was crowned with martyrdom, and was there buried by the Christians. On the same day likewise, in the time of the Emperor Aurelian, for the same faith, Symphorian, a youth of Autun, was variously tortured. Who, while he was being led unto the last punishment, hearing his mother crying out thus: “Son, son, remember eternal life, look up to heaven, and behold Him that reigneth there: for life is not taken from thee, but is changed into a better”: bravely offered his neck unto the executioner for the sake of Jesus Christ.
