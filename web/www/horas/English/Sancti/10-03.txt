[Officium]
Saint Thérèse of the Child Jesus, Virgin

[Oratio]
O Lord, who hast said: Unless you be converted and become as little children, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven: grant us, we beseech Thee, so to follow the footsteps of the holy Virgin Thérèse in humility and simplicity of heart, that we may attainthe eternal rewards.
$Qui vivis

[Lectio4]
Thérèse of the Child Jesus was born at Alençon in France of honourable parents, conspicuous for singular and assiduous piety toward God. From her earliest age, prevented [anticipated] by the divine Spirit, she desired to lead a religious life. But she seriously promised that she would deny nothing to God which He should seem to ask of her: which promise she strove faithfully to keep even unto death. In the fifth year of her age, her mother having been lost, she committed herself wholly to the providence of God under the watchful custody of her most loving father and of her sisters older in birth: by which teachers Thérèse, as a giant, exulted to run the way of perfection. At nine years of age she was given to be educated by the virgins of the Order of Saint Benedict at Lisieux, and there she was seen to excel in the knowledge of divine things. In the tenth year of her age a hidden and grievous illness long tormented her, from which, as she herself relates, she was divinely delivered by the help of the most Blessed Virgin, who appeared to her smiling, and whom under the title of Our Lady of Victories she strove to invoke through novenas. Then, filled with angelic fervour, she took care most diligently to prepare herself for the sacred banquet in which Christ is received.

[Lectio5]
As soon as she was first refreshed with the eucharistic bread, she was seen to draw in an insatiable hunger for this heavenly food: whence, as if inspired, she asked Jesus that He would turn every worldly consolation into bitterness for her. Then, glowing with a most tender love for Christ the Lord and for the Church, she held nothing more ancient than to enter the Order of Discalced Carmelites, that by her self-abnegation and her sacrifices she might bring help to priests, missionaries, and the whole Church, and might gain countless souls for Jesus Christ: which she promised she would do before God when already near death. On account of the defect of age, she encountered many difficulties for embracing the religious life, which nevertheless having overcome with incredible fortitude of soul, at fifteen years of age she happily entered the Carmel of Lisieux. There God disposed wonderful ascents in the heart of Thérèse, who, having imitated the hidden life of the Virgin Mary, as a watered garden put forth the flowers of all the virtues, but especially of exceeding charity toward God and toward neighbor.

[Lectio6]
That she might please the Most High the more, when she had read that admonition in the Sacred Scriptures: If any one is a little one, let him come to me; she wished to be a little one in spirit, and thence with filial trust she surrendered herself perpetually to God, as to a most loving Father. This way of spiritual childhood, according to the doctrine of the Gospel, she taught to others, especially to the novices, whom by obedience she undertook to form them in the study of religious virtues; and thus, filled with apostolic zeal, she opened the path of evangelical simplicity to a world filled with pride and a love of vanities. Moreover, her Spouse Jesus thoroughly inflamed her with the desire of suffering, both in soul and in body. Moreover, perceiving the charity of God neglected on every side, affected with the greatest sorrow, two years before her death she offered herself as a victim to the merciful love of God. Then, as she herself relates, she was wounded by a flame of heavenly fire: whence, consumed by charity, rapt into ecstasy, most fervently repeating: My God, I love Thee; at twenty-four years of age, on the thirtieth day of September, in the year one thousand eight hundred and ninety-seven, she flew away to her Spouse. But what she had promised when dying, that she would send down a perpetual rain of roses upon the earth, this, received into heaven, she has in very deed fulfilled by innumerable miracles and fulfils day by day. Whom Pius the Eleventh, Supreme Pontiff, having enrolled among the Blessed Virgins, and two years later, the greatest jubilee recurring, having placed among the Saints, constituted and declared the particular Patroness of all Missions.

Homily of Saint Leo the Pope
!Sermon 37, on the solemnity of the Epiphany 7, chapters 3-4
The whole discipline of Christian wisdom, dearly beloved, consists not in the abundance of word, not in the subtlety of disputing, nor in the appetite of praise and glory, but in true and voluntary humility, which the Lord Jesus Christ from the womb of His Mother even unto the punishment of the Cross chose and taught for all fortitude. For, when His disciples were inquiring among themselves, as the evangelist says, who of them should be greater in the kingdom of heaven, He called a little one, and set him in the midst of them, and said: Amen, I say to you, unless you be converted and become as little ones, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven. Whosoever therefore shall humble himself as this little child, he shall be the greater in the kingdom of heaven. Christ loves infancy, which first He undertook both in soul and in body. Christ loves infancy, the mistress of humility, the rule of innocence, the form of meekness. Christ loves infancy, to which He directs the morals of elders, to which He reduces the ages of old men; and inclines them to His own example, whom He exalts to the eternal kingdom.

[Lectio8]
But that we may be able fully to recognize how such a wondrous conversion can be apprehended and by what change we must return to the childlike degree, let blessed Paul teach us and say: Do not become children in sense, but in malice be little ones. Therefore we are not to return to the sports of infancy and our imperfect beginnings, but something is to be taken thence which may become even serious years, so that the transit of commotions may be swift, the return to peace speedy: let there be no memory of offense, no desire of dignity; the love of social communion, natural equality. For it is a great good not to know how to do harm and not to relish malicious things; because to inflict and return injury is the prudence of this world; but to render to no man evil for evil is the infancy of Christian equanimity.

[Lectio9]
To this likeness of little ones, dearly beloved, the mystery of today's festivity invites you; and this form of humility the child Savior, adored by the Magi, insinuates to you: who, that He might show what glory He prepared for His imitators, consecrated those born at the time of His birth by martyrdom; so that, born in Bethlehem, where Christ was born, they might become through the communion of age companions of His passion. Humility therefore is to be loved, and all elation is to be shunned by the faithful. Let one prefer another to himself, and let no one seek what is his own, but what is another's; so that, when the affection of benevolence shall have abounded in all, the poison of envy may be found in none: because he that exalteth himself shall be humbled, and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted, Jesus Christ our Lord Himself bearing witness, who with the Father and the Holy Ghost liveth and reigneth God, world without end. Amen.
