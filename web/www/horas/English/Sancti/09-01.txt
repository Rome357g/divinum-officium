[Officium]
Saint Giles, Abbot

[Name]
Giles

[Lectio93]
Giles of Athens, of royal stock, from his earliest age was so given to divine letters and the offices of charity, that he seemed to care for nothing else. And so, his parents being dead, he distributed his whole patrimony among the poor; nay more, he even put off his tunic, that he might cover a sick and needy man, who, being clothed therewith, immediately recovered. But thereafter becoming more renowned by many miracles, and fearing the celebrity of his name, he hastened unto blessed Caesarius at Arles. From whom departing after two years, he withdrew into a desert; where for a long time he lived in admirable sanctity upon the roots of herbs and the milk of a hind, which came unto him at fixed hours. Which hind, one day when the royal hounds were pursuing her, having taken refuge in the cave of Giles, moved the king of Gaul [Visigoths?] to ask of him with most earnest prayers that he would suffer a monastery to be built in the place of the cave. Whose administration, at the urgent request of the king, he undertook unwillingly; and having exercised that office for some years with prudence and piety, he passed unto heaven.
